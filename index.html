Pulse energy against repetition rate as reported. This glass in colour, other glasses grey; hollow marks = energy from average power ÷ rate. Shaded: output of our — (— nm, ≥ — fs, —–— kHz, ≤ — mW), before beam-path losses.

No paper for this glass states both repetition rate and pulse energy (or power).

With our laser compares each paper’s writing laser with what our CARBIDE can deliver (1025 nm, 208 fs – 7.8 ps, 60–1008 kHz, average power ≤ 5.1 W so pulse energy ≤ 5.1 W ÷ rate, at most 85 µJ):

  • Within our range wavelength, pulse duration, repetition rate and energy are all reachable. The paper’s recipe is a direct starting point.
  • Adapt recipe the glass is fine, but the paper used another wavelength (e.g. 800 nm Ti:sapphire, 515 nm green) or pulses shorter than our 208 fs. Nonlinear absorption and focal volume change with λ and τ, so the energy and speed must be found again with our own parameter scan.
  • Outside our range the paper needs a repetition rate above 1008 kHz or more pulse energy than we have at that rate.
  • Not enough data the paper (or the part we could read) does not state λ, τ or f.

“Why” lists what differs (λ, τ, f, E) or what is missing. Rates below 60 kHz are possible only through the pulse picker. Energies refer to the laser output; at the sample they are lower by the transmission of the beam path and objective.

    How to read the dashboard

    • Glasses opens on a catalog: one card per glass, grouped by family. Filter by optical class (linear/passive, nonlinear χ(3), active gain glass, mid-IR) or type a name. Click a card to open that glass’s page; “← All glasses” goes back.
    • Glass page, top: family, name, optical class, trade names and a one-paragraph summary. The sidebar “At a glance” lists key properties (each links to its datasheet or paper) and the full property table.
    • How it responds to the laser: sign and size of the index change (positive core, or negative so that a depressed cladding or double line is needed), the regime that works (low-rate athermal or MHz heat accumulation), best reported loss. “Full research notes” holds the complete notes behind each statement.
    • Writing parameters: the chart puts every paper at its repetition rate and pulse energy (log–log). Coloured dots belong to the open glass, grey dots to other glasses, hollow dots have energy computed as average power ÷ rate. The shaded area is what our CARBIDE can reach. The table lists each paper’s λ, τ, f, E, P, NA, writing speed, depth, Δn and loss, and the column “With our laser”.
    • With our laser: Within our range = λ within 50 nm of 1025 nm, τ between 208 fs and 7.8 ps, f between 60 and 1008 kHz, and E below our maximum at that rate, so the recipe is a direct starting point. Adapt recipe = other wavelength or shorter pulses, so energy and speed need our own scan. Outside our range = rate or energy we cannot reach. Not enough data = the paper does not state enough.
    • Papers and experiments: title (links to the paper), authors and journal, the experiment in one or two sentences, parameter chips, and badges: experiment type, where the details were read (green full text, amber abstract, red secondary source), and “Glass not confirmed” when the exact glass grade is uncertain. “Source notes” shows the laser model, focusing optics, conditions and the quoted evidence.
    • Where to buy: supplier cards, German suppliers first. Each shows product, published price with the date it was seen (or “on request”), formats and cutting/polishing services.
    • Other tabs: All papers (one sortable, searchable table across all glasses), Compare (one row per glass), Research groups (key groups, their glasses, lasers and papers).

    How this was collected

    • Literature search per glass family. Writing parameters are taken from the paper itself; an empty cell means not stated or not accessible, never estimated.
    • Some glasses were added because suppliers sell them for this work and got a separate, targeted literature search afterwards. A glass that still has 0 papers says so under “Still to check”.
    • Text is shown in two depths: a short, edited summary up front, and the complete research notes under “Full research notes” (per glass) and “Source notes” (per paper).
    • DOIs were matched through search results and publisher listings, not through Crossref. Check a DOI before citing it.
    • Prices come from public shop pages on the date shown, excluding VAT unless stated. Ask for a quote before ordering.
